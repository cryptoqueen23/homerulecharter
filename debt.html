Who can borrow money for Copperas Cove?

The difference between general obligation bonds, certificates of obligation, revenue bonds and refunding bonds, and which ones residents vote on.

The kinds of borrowing, in plain English

General Obligation Bond

Money borrowed by the city and generally repaid using tax-supported revenues. Some general obligation bonds require voter approval depending on the legal structure.

Certificate of Obligation

A borrowing method available to Texas cities that in many cases does not require a bond election. It carries notice requirements, and residents have a petition right that can force an election in some circumstances. The specifics are set by state law and should be checked against the current statute.

Revenue Bond

Money borrowed and repaid from the income of a specific city system, such as water or sewer, rather than from general taxes.

Refunding Bond

New borrowing issued to pay off older borrowing, usually to lower the interest cost. This is a refinancing rather than new spending.

Other municipal debt

Texas cities also use tax notes, lease-purchase arrangements and similar instruments. Each has its own rules about notice and voter involvement.

Legal interpretation may require additional review

Texas municipal debt law is detailed and the rules turn on the specific instrument used. Nothing on this page should be relied on for a legal conclusion about a particular issuance. Every statement here needs to be checked against the current statute and the actual bond documents.
